CF Industries, JERA and Mitsui break ground on Blue Point One — world's largest low-carbon ammonia plant, ~2.3 Mtpa CO₂ to be stored by 1PointFive

Hydrocarbon Engineering · 27 Aug 2026 · [North America]

The joint venture of CF Industries (40%), JERA (35%) and Mitsui & Co. (25%) broke ground in Ascension Parish, Louisiana on Blue Point One, a ~US$3.7bn autothermal-reforming (ATR) ammonia plant billed as the world's largest at 1.4 Mtpa, with roughly 2.3 Mtpa of CO₂ to be captured and permanently sequestered by Occidental's 1PointFive. First production is targeted for 2029 (FID was reached in April 2025; this week's news is the start of civil construction). The structure — two Japanese trading-house/utility investors anchoring a Gulf Coast blue-ammonia plant tied to dedicated US CO₂ storage — is a clear marker of how Asian offtake demand is underwriting large-scale North American carbon storage.

Sarawak reaffirms Kasawari as its first CCUS project, targeting start-up by 2028

DayakDaily · 27 Aug 2026 · [APAC]

Malaysian regional press reported that the PETRONAS-operated Kasawari CCS project offshore Sarawak (Block SK316, ~200 km off Bintulu) is being positioned as the state's first CCUS project, designed to strip and reinject about 3.3 Mtpa of CO₂ from the unusually high-CO₂ (~40%) Kasawari gas into a depleted offshore reservoir, with operations targeted by 2028 — though PETRONAS has separately signalled it is working to bring first injection forward to 2027. Sarawak's state energy firm PETROS and Mitsubishi have been linked to the wider Sarawak CCUS build-out. Kasawari is among the world's largest offshore CCS projects and a direct APAC comparator for Australia's offshore-storage and cross-border CO₂-hub ambitions.

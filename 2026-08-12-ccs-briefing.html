Cardinal Glass to install “world’s first” float-glass carbon capture at Winlock, Washington

gasworld · 11 Aug 2026 · [North America]

Cardinal Glass Industries announced it will integrate a post-combustion carbon-capture system at its Cardinal FG float-glass plant in Winlock, Washington — billed as the first such installation on a float-glass line. The system is designed to remove up to 95% of CO₂ from targeted process streams and capture roughly 130,000 tonnes of CO₂ a year, following feasibility, design and engineering work with technology partner K2-CO₂ of Padova, Italy. Cardinal says the project is entirely privately funded, with purchase orders issued and deposits paid on long-lead items; operations are targeted for Q1 2029. A notable extension of CCS into a new hard-to-abate industrial sector, though still years from start-up.

Consumer Watchdog report brands CCS a “multi-billion-dollar boondoggle” and a public risk

Consumer Watchdog / PR Newswire · 11 Aug 2026 · [North America] · [NGO]

US advocacy group Consumer Watchdog released a report arguing that carbon capture and storage is “a bad bet for the public and the environment,” singling out California Resources Corporation and warning that gas-plant CCS has never operated at commercial scale and that projects lean on federal 45Q tax credits to stay viable. It cites CBO and IEEFA figures on low real-world capture rates. The CCS industry pushed back the same day (a Washington Examiner “Daily on Energy” segment carried the sector’s rebuttal). A social-licence signal — organised, quantified US opposition to CCS economics and safety of the kind Australian proponents increasingly face too.

IEEFA: carbon capture is failing the capital-discipline test

IEEFA · 11 Aug 2026 · [Global] · [NGO]

The Institute for Energy Economics and Financial Analysis published a commentary arguing that CCS projects repeatedly disappoint on the metrics investors care about — cost overruns, under-delivered capture rates and reliance on public subsidy — and that the sector is not meeting a basic capital-allocation bar. An investor-facing critique that reinforces the “poor capital discipline” framing now shadowing CCS funding debates, and directly relevant to how CO2CRC and its partners position project economics.

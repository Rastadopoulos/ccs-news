Enhance Energy breaks ground on Origins CCS Hub — set to be Canada's largest, with first storage in January 2027

BOE Report / World Oil / The Globe and Mail · 9 Sep 2026 · [North America]

Enhance Energy, together with the governments of Canada and Alberta, broke ground near Clive, Alberta on the Origins carbon capture and storage hub. Once operating — targeted for January 2027 — it is expected to be Canada's largest CCS project, initially storing up to 1.5 million tonnes of CO₂ a year (roughly equivalent to taking more than 500,000 cars off the road) with room to expand. The hub connects to the Alberta Carbon Trunk Line and to Enhance's existing Clive CCS site, which has permanently stored more than 9 million tonnes of CO₂ since 2020, and will initially serve industrial emitters in Alberta's Industrial Heartland across oil and gas, petrochemicals, cement and power. It is a useful benchmark for the shared transport-and-storage hub model CO2CRC tracks for CarbonNet and Moomba.

Euronews · 9 Sep 2026 · [Europe & UK]

A Euronews analysis pegged to the EU's continued backing of carbon capture — following the recent inauguration of the Yara Sluiskil cross-border project — sets out critics' argument that CCS is a "dangerous distraction" that risks "billions in taxpayers' money" and delays deeper climate action. Oracle commits up to US$1m to study carbon capture and storage at its Project Jupiter data centre in New Mexico

Carbon Herald / Oracle · 8 Sep 2026 · [North America]

Oracle announced up to US$1 million in research funding to assess the feasibility of capturing CO₂ from on-site Bloom Energy fuel cells at its Project Jupiter facility and storing it in New Mexico geology — its first publicised CCS investment, tied to a 100%-carbon-free-energy-by-2031 goal. Fortera and MLC sign development agreement for first full-scale commercial ReAct cement plant capturing lime-kiln CO₂

Carbon Herald / Global Cement · 8 Sep 2026 · [North America]

Fortera and MLC signed a development agreement to build a full-scale commercial plant producing more than 300,000 t/yr of low-carbon ReAct cement, using Fortera's ReCarb process to capture CO₂ from MLC's high-calcium lime kilns and mineralise it into cement (~50% captured CO₂ by weight), scaling up from a 15,000 t/yr Californian demo. Location, cost and timeline were not disclosed. link

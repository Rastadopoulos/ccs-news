Acorn CCS cluster left in funding limbo after UK Budget reprioritises its £200m grant

Scottish Construction Now / energy-pedia / ITV News · 29 Oct 2026 · [Europe & UK]

The £200m of carbon-capture capital grant funding pulled in the Autumn Budget was earmarked for the Acorn cluster in north-east Scotland (St Fergus), leaving the project's near-term funding uncertain. Acorn has been without a confirmed lead developer since Storegga stepped back as lead in December 2025; Storegga said the Budget "does not change Acorn's current development programme." Chief Secretary to the Treasury Emma Reynolds said the government "stands by" the £200m commitment and indicated future support could come as loans or equity rather than grants — a material shift in the funding model for a project long treated as the UK's "Track 2" flagship.

Ozona and Marubeni reach FID on Pawnee carbon-sequestration project in Texas

Offshore Technology / BIC Magazine · 29 Oct 2026 · [North America]

Ozona CCS and Japan's Marubeni Corporation announced a final investment decision on the Pawnee Carbon Sequestration Project, a 50:50 joint venture in Bee County, Texas (Eagle Ford basin). Ozona will build and operate the storage infrastructure while Marubeni leads commercialisation and marketing of the resulting carbon credits — a trading-house-led offtake model for US onshore storage. The partners said the project moves Ozona from development into construction and can expand as additional CO₂ sources connect; headline capacity and capex were not disclosed in the trade-press reports seen this run.

UK Autumn Budget pulls £200m from carbon-capture grants; Viking grant kept, Acorn hit

The Chemical Engineer / Carbon Brief / Energy Voice · 29 Oct 2026 · [Europe & UK]

The UK Autumn Budget (28 October) reprioritised £200m of carbon-capture capital grants to help fund higher defence spending — framed by trade press as part of a roughly £700m reduction across CCUS and hydrogen support combined. The Budget maintained grant funding for the Viking cluster (Humber) and continued to prioritise the Track-1 Teesside, Merseyside (HyNet) and North Wales projects, while the pulled £200m was the money earmarked for Acorn. Ministers said the commitment still stands and signalled a possible shift from grants to loans or equity stakes. The move is a notable cooling in UK public CCS support and a reminder of how exposed grant-dependent clusters are to fiscal reprioritisation.

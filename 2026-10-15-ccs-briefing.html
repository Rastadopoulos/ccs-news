GOV.UK · 14 Oct 2026 · [Europe/UK]

A UK government minister used a keynote speech, "Carbon capture and the renewal of British industry," to cast CCUS as "economic infrastructure, not just environmental" and as central to rebuilding the country's industrial base — the government's clearest rhetorical answer to industry warnings about policy and funding delays. Indonesia prepares incentives to spur carbon capture at coal-fired power plants

Petromindo · 14 Oct 2026 · [Aus/APAC]

Indonesia is preparing a package of incentives to encourage deployment of carbon capture technology at its coal-fired power plants (PLTU), extending a CCS push that until now has centred on upstream oil-and-gas and dedicated storage licences (under Presidential Regulation 14/2024) into the power sector that dominates its grid. The move operationalises the CCS-for-coal roadmap flagged in the National Electricity Plan and is a notable signal that a major Asian coal economy is leaning on CCS retrofit rather than only early plant retirement. A high-marginal-value, non-Western policy catch: Indonesia is a priority APAC CCS market and a plausible cross-border storage and partnership counterpart for Australia, so a power-sector incentive signal shapes regional CCS demand.

Japan pushes to move carbon capture from demonstration to a commercial industry

Energynews.pro · 14 Oct 2026 · [Aus/APAC]

A trade report frames Japan as working to take CCS out of the demonstration phase and into a commercial industry — consistent with its roadmap to begin commercial CCS around 2030, the JOGMEC "Advanced CCS" projects (several built around ship-transported liquefied CO₂) now advancing toward investment decisions, and the licensing regime created by the 2024 Act on Carbon Dioxide Storage Business. The commercialisation pace matters for the Asia-Pacific CO₂ value chain, including proposed cross-border CO₂ shipping from Japan to storage sites in partner countries. For CO2CRC, Japan is a central actor in APAC cross-border CO₂ shipping and a potential source of stored-CO₂ demand for Australian basins, so its move toward commercial scale directly affects Australian storage-hub prospects.

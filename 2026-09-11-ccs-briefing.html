Climeworks reports its Mammoth DAC plant captured six times more CO₂ in H1 2026 as running costs more than halve

Bloomberg · 10 Sep 2026 · [Europe]

Swiss carbon-removal firm Climeworks said its Mammoth direct-air-capture plant in Iceland removed 675 tonnes of CO₂ in the first half of 2026 — almost six times the volume of the same period a year earlier — while the cost of running the plant fell by more than half (the company did not disclose the absolute figure). It attributed the gains to upgrades to the plant design and to improvements in the filter (sorbent) material. The absolute tonnage remains modest, but the combination of a step-change in throughput and a halving of operating cost is the signal worth watching for DAC's cost-down curve and for the credibility of durable carbon-dioxide-removal credits.

Vateris secures over €50m in binding offtakes and LOIs for CO₂-derived carbon-negative minerals

Carbon Herald · 10 Sep 2026 · [Europe]

UK climate-tech firm Vateris signed binding multi-year offtake agreements and letters of intent worth over €50m ($55–58m) in potential annual turnover with six industrial partners. Its process mineralises captured industrial flue-gas CO₂ into two product lines: engineered carbon-negative calcium carbonate (GypCarb, for construction), with Holcim, Marshalls and Goldbeck committing to buy; and high-purity sulphate of potash (fertiliser), with Waypoint Commodities and two unnamed producers committing. First deliveries are targeted from a UK demonstration plant from late 2027, with a full-scale European plant planned for 2029 (FID pending). The deal is a demand-side signal for CO₂-utilisation/mineralisation offtakes and for CBAM-driven European low-carbon supply chains.

Finland awards €26.6m to three industrial biogenic carbon-capture (BECCS) projects

Finnish Government / Carbon Pulse · 9 Sep 2026 · [Europe]

Finland's Ministry of Economic Affairs and Employment awarded €26.6m in state investment aid to three industrial biogenic carbon-capture projects — the largest tranche, €21m, to Metsä Fibre for a 100,000 t/yr biogenic CO₂ capture unit at its Rauma pulp mill — the first awards under a €90m competitive scheme, with the captured biogenic CO₂ destined largely as feedstock for synthetic e-fuels. This binding award follows the 4 September Ministerial Finance Committee endorsement noted in Tuesday's briefing, and is carried here because its 9 September decision date sits just outside today's window.

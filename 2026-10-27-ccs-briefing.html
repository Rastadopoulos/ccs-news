CSIRO runs sunlight-driven direct air capture field trial at its WA In-Situ Laboratory, timed for GHGT-18

Energy News Bulletin · 26 Oct 2026 · [Australia]

CSIRO is running a month-long field demonstration of Seattle start-up Banyu Carbon's sunlight-driven direct air capture (DAC) unit at its In-Situ Laboratory (ISL) in Western Australia's south-west, with the trade press describing it as the first time a carbon-capture (rather than storage) technology has been deployed at the ISL site. Banyu's process uses light-sensitive photoacid compounds to release CO2 from a capture solution without the thermal energy conventional DAC requires — chemistry CSIRO first demonstrated at its Newcastle Energy Centre in 2013. The fridge-sized unit, shipped from Seattle in September and said to be 10–20× larger than Banyu's earlier prototypes, was set up to run for delegates at GHGT-18 (Perth, 25–29 October); captured CO2 is vented during this trial rather than stored.

GHGT-18 opens in Perth with 1,000+ delegates — CO2CRC among the co-hosts

CSIRO · 26 Oct 2026 · [Australia]

The 18th Greenhouse Gas Control Technologies Conference (GHGT-18) — the sector's flagship global scientific meeting, run by the IEA Greenhouse Gas R&D Programme — opened at Crown Perth, drawing more than 1,000 delegates over 25–29 October. It is the first time the quadrennial-scale event has been held in Western Australia, with CSIRO, CO2CRC, Geoscience Australia and Australian Energy Producers among the co-hosts. The programme spans demonstration projects, capture and storage technology, MMV, and the evolving regulatory landscape, and is the backdrop to several Australian technology demonstrations this week (including the CSIRO/Banyu DAC trial above).

Arbon Earth secures first marine-CDR permit in Indonesia

Carbon Herald · 30 Sep 2026 · [APAC / Indonesia]

Swedish climate-tech firm Arbon Earth has won what it describes as a first-of-its-kind Indonesian permit to run deep-sea marine carbon-dioxide removal (mCDR), working directly with Indonesia’s Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries (KKP) on a phased scale-up. Its approach cultivates seaweed that draws down atmospheric CO₂ and then sinks the biomass to the deep ocean floor; testing will start off Lombok Island, alongside the company’s existing Kenya operations. The permit lifts its allowable production rate and is aimed at meeting larger contracted CO₂-removal purchases. Marine CDR sits outside conventional geological CCS, but the regulatory first is a notable signal of Indonesia widening its CO₂-removal governance.

NYK buys 30% stake in Vår Energi’s Trudvang offshore CO₂ storage project

Carbon Herald · 30 Sep 2026 · [Europe-UK / Norway · Japan]

Japanese shipping major Nippon Yusen Kabushiki Kaisha (NYK) is acquiring a 30% interest in Vår Energi’s Trudvang CCS partnership and the corresponding EXL 007 offshore storage licence in the Norwegian North Sea — its first direct equity investment in geological CO₂ storage. Trudvang, in the Utsira formation adjacent to Equinor’s Sleipner site, targets an initial 2 Mtpa of injection with expansion potential beyond 10 Mtpa. Subject to Norwegian Ministry of Energy approval, ownership becomes operator Vår Energi (40%), INPEX Idemitsu Norge (30%) and NYK (30%). The move extends NYK’s liquefied-CO₂ shipping play (via its Knutsen NYK Carbon Carriers JV) into offshore handling, injection and storage — a further data-point in the capture-here / store-there value chain relevant to Australia-APAC cross-border models. (NYK issued its own release on 29 Sep; Tier-1 trade coverage carried the deal on 30 Sep.)
